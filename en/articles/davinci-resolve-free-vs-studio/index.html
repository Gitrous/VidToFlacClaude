The audio problem in the free version

One of the most frustrating limits of the free DaVinci Resolve — and the one that drives most people to look at Studio — is audio codec support, especially on Linux.

The free version does not include licences to decode certain patent-encumbered audio formats:

  • AAC — the standard in MP4 and MOV files from cameras, iPhones, GoPros and OBS. Does not work on Linux.
  • MP3 — does not work reliably on Linux.
  • E-AC-3 (Dolby Digital Plus) — does not work on any platform, not even in Studio.
  • DTS — limited or no support in the free Linux build.

The result is the familiar problem: you import a clip, the video looks perfect, but the audio shows up greyed out or simply is not there on the timeline.

An important caveat about Studio and Linux: DaVinci Resolve Studio improves audio support on Linux compared with the free version, but it does not fix it completely. E-AC-3 still does not work, and neither do some AAC profiles. Paying for Studio is no guarantee that all your files will have audio.

When Studio really is worth buying

That said, there are cases where Studio clearly justifies itself. These are the real reasons the outlay pays off:

1. AI noise reduction

Studio's DaVinci Neural Engine includes AI-based temporal and spatial noise reduction. It is noticeably better than any third-party plugin at the same price. If you shoot in low light with a camera whose sensor is not top-tier, this feature alone can justify Studio.

2. Resolutions above 4K UHD

If you work with cinema-camera footage — ARRI, RED, Blackmagic Cinema Camera 6K and above — the free version cannot handle those resolutions. Studio has no resolution limit.

3. GPU-accelerated H.265 export

The free version encodes H.265 in software, on the CPU, which can be extremely slow for long, high-resolution projects. Studio uses GPU acceleration — NVENC on NVIDIA, VCE on AMD, the Media Engine on Apple Silicon — cutting export times dramatically.

4. Team collaboration

If you work alongside other editors, colourists or audio engineers on the same project at the same time, you need Studio with a DaVinci server. The free version does not support real-time multi-user editing.

5. Magic Mask and AI tracking

Studio's AI masking and tracking tools (Magic Mask, object tracking) hugely speed up the rotoscoping and motion-tracking work you have to do by hand in the free version.

6. ProRes RAW

If you edit footage shot in ProRes RAW — an iPhone 15 Pro with a compatible app, Sony cameras with an external recorder — you need Studio to work with those files in DaVinci Resolve at all.

Price, and ways to get Studio

DaVinci Resolve Studio costs around 295 EUR / 295 USD as a one-off payment. There is no subscription: you pay once and get updates forever within the same major version (17, 18, 19 and so on). Major version upgrades have historically been free too, though Blackmagic does not guarantee that.

Ways to get it cheaper, or free:

  • Blackmagic Design capture cards: some bundle Studio for free (DeckLink, UltraStudio). If you need a capture card anyway, that can work out cheaper than buying Studio separately.
  • Blackmagic cameras: the Blackmagic Pocket Cinema Camera and other models include Studio with the purchase.
  • USB dongle: Studio comes with a USB dongle you can move between machines, which lets you share the licence in a controlled setting.
There is no monthly subscription: DaVinci Resolve Studio is sold only as a one-off purchase. There is no monthly or annual plan. Any "DaVinci Resolve Studio monthly" offer you come across is a third party, not Blackmagic.

Frequently asked questions

How much does DaVinci Resolve Studio cost?

DaVinci Resolve Studio costs around 295 EUR / 295 USD as a one-off payment, with no subscription. Updates within the same major version are free. It is also bundled with some Blackmagic Design cameras and capture cards.

Does the free version have a watermark or a time limit?

No watermark, no time limit. You can use the free DaVinci Resolve indefinitely and export video with no logo added. The restrictions are functional — no AI noise reduction, nothing above 4K UHD, no multi-user collaboration — not restrictions on use or time.

Does DaVinci Resolve Studio fix the AAC audio problem on Linux?

Partly. Studio ships more audio decoders on Linux than the free version, but it does not cover every case. E-AC-3 (Dolby Digital Plus) works in no edition, and neither do some AAC profiles. The most reliable fix, and a free one, is to convert the audio to FLAC with VidToFLAC before importing.

Can I upgrade from free to Studio later on?

Yes. You can start on the free version and buy Studio whenever you like. Projects created in the free version are fully compatible with Studio — they simply open, and you carry on with every feature unlocked.

Does the free version export 4K without trouble?

Yes. The free DaVinci Resolve exports full 4K UHD (3840x2160) with no limits. What it does not support is GPU-accelerated H.265 export — CPU only, which is slower — or resolutions above 4K such as 5.7K, 6K or 8K.

Is there any colour quality difference between free and Studio?

Not in the colour engine itself. Both versions use the same 32-bit floating-point colour processing. Studio adds extra tools — AI noise reduction, AI object tracking and some Neural Engine-based Resolve FX — but the underlying colour quality is identical.
