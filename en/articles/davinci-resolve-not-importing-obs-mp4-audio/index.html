Why DaVinci Resolve will not import the audio

When you record on a phone, an action camera, a mirrorless or with OBS Studio, the resulting file carries audio compressed with a codec: almost always AAC, the standard in MP4 and camera MOV files, or MP3, common in screen recordings and older software.

The free DaVinci Resolve ships no AAC or MP3 decoders on Linux, because Blackmagic Design does not pay those patent licences for the Linux build. On Windows and macOS, AAC support exists, but certain container-and-codec combinations still fail, particularly with files from capture software or cameras set to non-standard options.

The usual symptoms: a greyed-out audio track in the Media Pool · Unsupported audio codec on import · a flat waveform · the clip imports but plays silently

The answer is not to reinstall DaVinci Resolve or go hunting for paid plugins. It is to change the file's audio codec to FLAC, which DaVinci Resolve supports natively on Windows, macOS and Linux.

As you can see, practically any video shot on consumer hardware arrives with AAC. On Linux the outcome is always the same: DaVinci Resolve sees the clip, but as far as it is concerned the audio does not exist.

The special case of OBS Studio

OBS Studio is the recording and streaming software creators use most, and by default it records MKV or MP4 with AAC audio at 160 kbps. That makes OBS the single most common source of this problem among editors working in DaVinci Resolve on Linux.

There are two variants of the OBS problem:

  • Recording to MKV: OBS recommends MKV for recording, because it survives crashes better, but AAC audio inside that MKV still will not work in DaVinci Resolve on Linux.
  • Recording to MP4: if you use the OBS "Remux to MP4" option afterwards, the file still has AAC — the container changes, the audio problem does not.
The immediate fix: you do not need to change your OBS workflow. Convert the recordings you already have with VidToFLAC — under 30 seconds for a 1–2 hour file — and the audio will be there in DaVinci Resolve.

Configuring OBS so it never happens again

If you would rather fix the problem at source, you can set OBS to record audio in a format DaVinci Resolve can always read:

Option A: record straight to FLAC from OBS

OBS 27 and later can record MKV with FLAC audio. Go to Settings → Output → Advanced mode → Recording tab:

  • Container: Matroska Video (.mkv)
  • Audio encoder: FLAC

With that set, your recordings will work in DaVinci Resolve from the outset.

Option B: record uncompressed PCM

PCM (WAV) is another lossless format DaVinci Resolve supports on Windows, macOS and Linux. The drawback is size: a two-hour recording can produce several gigabytes of audio alone. FLAC gives the same quality in 40–60% less space.

Option C: convert after recording, leaving OBS alone

If you would rather not change your OBS settings, or you already have a pile of older AAC recordings, use VidToFLAC to convert them. It is the most practical option when dozens of files have built up.

Alternative: an FFmpeg command in the terminal

If you prefer the command line, this is the exact equivalent of what VidToFLAC does — copy the video without recompressing and convert only the audio to FLAC:

ffmpeg -i obs-recording.mkv -c:v copy -c:a flac output.mkv

To convert several files at once in bash:

for f in *.mkv; do ffmpeg -i "$f" -c:v copy -c:a flac "flac_${f}"; done

If the video uses a codec the output MKV container will not take with -c:v copy — rare, but possible with certain H.265 profiles — use -c:v libx264 -preset ultrafast -crf 18 instead of -c:v copy.

Frequently asked questions

Why does DaVinci Resolve play no audio from an MP4?

MP4 files from smartphones, cameras and OBS use AAC or MP3 for audio. The free DaVinci Resolve on Linux ships no decoders for those codecs. The fix is to convert the audio to FLAC with VidToFLAC: the video is copied losslessly and the audio becomes readable on Windows, macOS and Linux.

Why does OBS record audio DaVinci Resolve cannot read?

OBS uses AAC as its default audio codec, because it is compact and widely compatible with players and streaming platforms. The problem is that DaVinci Resolve on Linux cannot decode it. You can set OBS to record FLAC (Settings → Output → Audio encoder → FLAC), or simply convert the recordings you have with VidToFLAC.

Do I lose video quality by converting the audio to FLAC?

No. VidToFLAC does a remux: the video stream is copied bit for bit, never decoded or recompressed. Every frame of the resulting file is identical to the original. Only the audio changes from AAC to FLAC — and FLAC is lossless, so the audio quality is identical too.

Does this fix work in DaVinci Resolve on Windows and macOS too?

Yes. On Windows and macOS DaVinci Resolve does support AAC, but there are cases where it fails: files with non-standard AAC profiles, unusual container combinations, E-AC-3 and so on. Converting to FLAC works on every operating system and rules out any audio codec incompatibility.

How long does the conversion take with VidToFLAC?

Very little. Since the video is copied without recompression and the audio is usually under 5% of the file size, converting a two-hour recording typically takes 10 to 30 seconds. The only limiting factor is your disk's write speed.

Are my files uploaded to any server?

Never. VidToFLAC uses FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, which runs inside your own browser. The file never leaves your device: no upload, no intermediate server, no sign-up. You can even disconnect from the internet once the page has loaded and the conversion will still work.
