Why DaVinci Resolve will not play the audio

DaVinci Resolve is a professional video editor, but it does not ship every audio decoder in existence. The reason is straightforward: many popular audio codecs — AAC, MP3, AC-3 Dolby Digital — are patent-protected and require licence payments. Blackmagic Design, the company behind DaVinci Resolve, includes those licences in the Windows and macOS builds, but not in the free Linux build.

On top of that, even on Windows and macOS there are container formats and particular codec combinations DaVinci Resolve handles poorly, especially in files from stills cameras, drones and smartphones.

The outcome is always the same: the clip appears on the timeline, the video plays, but there is no sound. Or the clip simply shows as "Media offline".

PCM/WAV works too, but takes far more space since it is uncompressed. FLAC gives the same quality as PCM while compressing by 40 to 60% — it is the better choice in every scenario.

The most common audio errors in DaVinci Resolve, and what they mean

1. Silent audio with no error message

The clip imports fine and the waveform appears on the timeline, but nothing is audible on playback. This usually happens with AAC on Linux or Opus inside WebM files. DaVinci Resolve can see the audio track but cannot decode it.

The diagnostic sign: in the media inspector (Media Pool → right-click → Clip Attributes), the audio track is listed but shows 0 active channels, or the codec is flagged in red.

2. "Unsupported audio codec"

DaVinci Resolve states outright that it cannot decode the audio. The message appears during import in the Media Pool, or when you try to add the clip to the timeline. It is the most direct form of the problem.

The usual messages: Unsupported audio codec · Audio codec not supported · Cannot decode audio stream

3. "Media offline" on the audio clip

The clip appears on a red background with the "Media offline" message on the timeline. It is usually associated with files that have been moved or renamed, but it also happens when DaVinci Resolve rejects the file outright over a codec incompatibility.

4. Audio out of sync

The audio does play, but runs ahead of or behind the picture. This happens frequently with Variable Frame Rate (VFR) files, typical of smartphone video. The camera varies the frame rate scene by scene, which confuses DaVinci Resolve's sync engine.

5. A flat or missing waveform

The audio track exists on the timeline but the waveform is completely flat, with no peaks or troughs. It means DaVinci Resolve imported the track but could not read the audio data.

DaVinci Resolve on Linux: why AAC audio will not play

DaVinci Resolve on Linux deserves its own section, because it is where most people hit audio problems, and the reason is structural: Blackmagic Design does not include the AAC, MP3 and other codec patent licences in the Linux build. This is not a bug; it is a licensing decision.

What that means in practice:

  • An MP4 shot on an iPhone (AAC) will play the video but not the audio.
  • A MOV from a Sony or Canon camera — also AAC — has the same problem.
  • Videos downloaded from YouTube (Opus inside WebM) will have no audio either.
  • Screen recordings with MP3 audio will not work either.
The definitive Linux fix: convert your videos' audio to FLAC before importing them into DaVinci Resolve. FLAC is open source, carries no patent licences, and DaVinci Resolve supports it natively on every platform, Linux included.

Some people try to solve this by installing extra codec packages system-wide — ffmpeg, gstreamer, the davinci-resolve-studio package — but those workarounds are partial, distribution-specific and liable to break on update. The robust answer is to convert the file before importing it.

Video formats with audio problems in DaVinci Resolve

MP4 from an iPhone or Android phone

Smartphone video almost always uses AAC for audio and H.264 or H.265 for video. On Linux, AAC does not work in DaVinci Resolve. The fix is an MP4-to-MKV remux with FLAC audio, leaving the video untouched.

MOV from Sony, Canon and Fuji cameras

Mirrorless and DSLR cameras usually record MOV with AAC or 24-bit PCM audio. AAC causes trouble on Linux; 24-bit PCM can cause trouble in particular DaVinci Resolve combinations. Converting MOV to FLAC resolves both.

WebM from YouTube or screen recorders

WebM uses Opus or Vorbis for audio. Neither has reliable support in DaVinci Resolve. If you download YouTube videos or record your screen to WebM, you will need to convert the WebM to FLAC.

MKV with AAC or AC-3 audio

MKV is only a container: it can hold any audio codec. An MKV with AAC audio has exactly the same problem as an MP4 with AAC. You can fix it with an MKV-to-FLAC remux: the container stays MKV, but the audio becomes FLAC.

Video with E-AC-3 (Dolby Digital Plus) audio

E-AC-3 is the audio format behind a lot of streaming content (Amazon Prime, Netflix). If you edit that kind of material, note that E-AC-3 works in DaVinci Resolve on no platform at all. Converting to FLAC is the only way.

Frequently asked questions about audio in DaVinci Resolve

Why won't DaVinci Resolve play my videos' audio?

DaVinci Resolve does not ship decoders for every audio codec. The most troublesome are AAC, MP3, AC-3, E-AC-3 and Opus, especially on Linux where the free version's codec support is very limited. The fix is to convert the audio to FLAC before importing.

Does converting the audio to FLAC cost me picture quality?

No. A remux copies the video stream bit for bit, without recompressing. The frames are identical to the original. Only the audio is re-encoded, to FLAC, which is lossless — so the audio quality is identical too.

Can I convert a camera MOV to MKV with FLAC without trouble?

Yes. The container changes from MOV to MKV, but the contents are the same. DaVinci Resolve works perfectly well with MKV. The only noticeable difference is that the resulting file may be slightly larger, since FLAC compresses losslessly and therefore less than lossy AAC.

Why doesn't my iPhone video audio work in DaVinci Resolve on Linux?

iPhone video uses AAC for audio. DaVinci Resolve on Linux cannot decode AAC, because Blackmagic Design does not include that patent licence in the Linux build. The fix is to convert the audio to FLAC with VidToFLAC before importing the clip.

Is there any way to fix the audio without converting the file?

On Linux, not reliably. Some distributions let you install extra decoder packages, but those workarounds are fragile and tied to particular OS versions. Converting to FLAC is the only robust approach that works on every system.

What is the difference between FLAC and WAV for DaVinci Resolve?

Both are lossless and both work well in DaVinci Resolve. The difference is size: FLAC compresses the audio — typically 40–60% smaller than WAV — without discarding any data. On long videos that difference is significant, which is why we recommend FLAC over WAV.
