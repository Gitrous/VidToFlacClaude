MOV vs MP4: what actually differs

MOV is Apple's container format, created for QuickTime. MP4 is the international standard derived from MPEG-4. Both are containers: they do not determine video quality, only how the video, audio and subtitle tracks are packaged.

What matters is the codec inside the container:

Property MOV MP4
Developed by Apple (QuickTime) MPEG / ISO
Compatibility Very good on macOS/iOS; patchy elsewhere Universal: any device, platform or editor
Typical video codecs H.264, H.265, ProRes, MJPEG H.264, H.265, AV1
Typical audio codecs AAC, PCM, FLAC, AC-3 AAC, MP3, AC-3
DaVinci Resolve (Linux) AAC audio does not work AAC audio does not work
With FLAC audio Supported (in MKV) Supported

The conclusion: if you only change the container from MOV to MP4 without touching the codecs, picture and audio quality are identical. The video and audio streams are preserved without re-encoding; the container, however, is rewritten, so the file is not byte-for-byte identical.

What a remux is and why it loses nothing

A remux (remultiplexing) is the operation of changing the file's envelope without touching what is inside. The H.264 video that was in the MOV is copied across to the MP4 exactly as it was — not a single frame is decoded or recompressed.

That is radically different from a re-encode, where the video is decompressed frame by frame and compressed again with a codec. Every recompression cycle introduces quality loss: blocking artefacts, loss of fine detail, colour degradation. The more times you do it, the worse it gets.

The golden rule: if the original video is already H.264 or H.265 — which is the case for practically any MOV from an iPhone, a GoPro or a mirrorless camera — remuxing to MP4 costs zero quality. Loss only happens in a re-encode, and a re-encode is only necessary when the codecs are not MP4-compatible.

When a MOV → MP4 remux works straight away

For the great majority of MOV files — those shot on an iPhone, a GoPro, a Sony/Canon/Fuji/Nikon camera, or by capture software — remuxing to MP4 works without any trouble, because the codecs are directly compatible with the MP4 container:

  • H.264 video → compatible with MP4 ✓
  • H.265 (HEVC) video → compatible with MP4 ✓
  • AAC audio → compatible with MP4 ✓
  • Stereo PCM audio → compatible with MP4 ✓

If your MOV came from any of these sources, the remux is instant and lossless:

When it does not work, and what to do

A direct remux to MP4 fails when the MOV contains codecs the MP4 container will not take:

  • ProRes (4444, 422, LT, Proxy) — Apple's editing codec; it does not fit in MP4. It needs a re-encode to H.264 or H.265, or you keep it in MOV/MKV.
  • FLAC inside MOV — FLAC is not compatible with the MP4 container. The audio has to be converted to AAC or PCM.
  • Multichannel audio beyond stereo in non-standard formats — some MOV files from professional cameras carry multichannel audio that does not sit well in MP4.
  • MJPEG (Motion JPEG) — some older cameras record in this format. It technically fits in MP4, but it can cause problems.
Got a cinema-camera MOV with ProRes? Then do not target MP4 — ProRes is an editing codec, and re-encoding it to H.264 would genuinely cost you quality. Keep the file as MOV, or export it to MKV. To edit it in DaVinci Resolve on Linux, convert only the audio to FLAC with VidToFLAC.

MOV by source: iPhone, GoPro, cameras

iPhone and iPad

iPhone MOV files carry H.264 video (up to the iPhone 12) or H.265 (iPhone 12 onwards in efficiency mode) with AAC audio. The remux to MP4 is direct. If you are heading into DaVinci Resolve on Linux afterwards, convert the audio to FLAC as well — AAC will not play in the free Linux build.

GoPro HERO

Modern GoPro models record to MP4 by default, but some can record to MOV. The video is usually H.265 (GoPro HEVC) with AAC audio. Remuxing to MP4 works without trouble.

Sony, Canon, Fujifilm, Nikon

Mirrorless and DSLR cameras from these makers record to MOV (or MP4) with H.264 or H.265 and AAC or stereo PCM audio. The remux is direct. The exception is models recording in XAVC (Sony) or high-quality modes with proprietary internal encoding — in those cases VidToFLAC detects automatically whether anything extra is needed.

GoPro MAX and 360 cameras

360 videos in MOV can carry multiple audio tracks (spatial stereo) that complicate the remux. If the result has only one audio track, that is expected — use the camera's own software to process the 360 footage before converting.

Converting MOV to MP4 with FFmpeg in the terminal

If you prefer the command line, FFmpeg is the standard tool. This command remuxes without touching any codec — ideal when the MOV carries H.264/H.265 and AAC:

ffmpeg -i input.mov -c copy output.mp4

If the audio is AAC and you want it as FLAC for compatibility with DaVinci Resolve on Linux:

ffmpeg -i input.mov -c:v copy -c:a flac output.mp4

If the MOV has ProRes and you need H.264 for players that cannot handle ProRes — accepting some quality loss, which is unavoidable when re-encoding ProRes:

ffmpeg -i input.mov -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -c:a flac output.mp4

To process a folder of MOV files in bash:

for f in *.mov; do
  ffmpeg -i "$f" -c:v copy -c:a flac "${f%.mov}.mp4"
done
Editing in DaVinci Resolve on Linux? After converting to MP4, check that the audio is no longer AAC. If the original MOV had AAC and you only ran -c copy, the resulting MP4 still has AAC — and DaVinci Resolve on Linux will not play that either. Use -c:a flac, or go through VidToFLAC to convert the audio to FLAC in the same step.

Frequently asked questions

Do you lose quality converting MOV to MP4?

No, not if it is done as a remux. A remux copies the video and audio bit for bit without recompressing. Quality is only lost in a re-encode, which is only necessary when the MOV contains codecs MP4 cannot hold, such as ProRes or FLAC.

How long does converting a MOV to MP4 take?

A remux is nearly instant: a 4 GB MOV takes between 10 and 30 seconds with VidToFLAC or FFmpeg, because the video is never decoded or re-encoded. Only the container changes.

Can I convert an iPhone MOV to MP4 without installing anything?

Yes. VidToFLAC runs directly in the browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari) with nothing to install. Drop in the iPhone MOV, press Convert and download the MP4. Everything happens on your device; nothing is uploaded.

What is the difference between MOV and MP4?

MOV is Apple's container format; MP4 is the international standard. Both can hold the same codecs — H.264, H.265, AAC. The difference is compatibility: MP4 works on practically any device and platform, whereas MOV is more tied to the Apple ecosystem.

Does VidToFLAC convert MOV to MP4?

Yes. VidToFLAC accepts MOV as input and can export to MP4. The video is copied without recompression and the audio is converted to FLAC, losslessly. The result is ideal for editing in DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro and other editors.

Does an iPhone MOV have AAC audio? Does it work in DaVinci Resolve?

iPhone MOV files carry AAC audio. When imported into DaVinci Resolve on Linux, that AAC audio will not play, because the free build ships no AAC decoder on Linux. Converting the MOV with VidToFLAC, so the audio becomes FLAC, solves the problem at no cost. See the full guide to audio errors in DaVinci Resolve.
