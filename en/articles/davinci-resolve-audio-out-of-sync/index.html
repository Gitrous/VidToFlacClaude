The most common causes of audio drift

Audio/video drift in DaVinci Resolve is rarely random: in most cases it comes down to one of these three causes, though they are not the only possibilities. The trouble is that the symptoms look alike even when the causes differ, so people often reach for the wrong fix — nudging the offset by hand on the timeline — without addressing the root.

Cause Main symptom Does it accumulate?
Incompatible audio codec Constant offset from the start of the clip Sometimes
Variable frame rate (VFR) Drift that grows through the clip Yes, always
Export mistake Fine in preview, wrong in the export Varies

Cause 1: an incompatible audio codec

Most common An audio codec with no native support

DaVinci Resolve, particularly on Linux, cannot natively decode codecs such as AAC, MP3, AC-3 or E-AC-3. When it forces the decode anyway, it introduces an audio delay that shows up as drift.

This is especially common with:

  • MP4 files shot on phones (iPhone, Android): they carry AAC audio, which DaVinci Resolve on Linux does not decode cleanly.
  • Camera MOV files: AAC or PCM audio at certain sample rates.
  • OBS Studio recordings: by default OBS records MP4 with AAC audio.
  • GoPro and drone MP4s: H.264/H.265 paired with AAC.

The classic symptom is that the offset stays constant through the clip rather than growing. On Linux it is more pronounced, because DaVinci Resolve Free ships no licensed AAC or MP3 decoders.

Cause 2: variable frame rate (VFR)

Very common Video with a variable frame rate

Phones, screen recorders and action cameras record at a frame rate that varies from frame to frame (VFR). DaVinci Resolve assumes a constant frame rate (CFR), so the video timestamps stop lining up with the audio and the drift grows as the clip runs on.

VFR (Variable Frame Rate) turns up very often in:

  • OBS screen captures: screen capture mode produces VFR by default when the content has no stable frame rate.
  • Phone video in efficiency modes: some Android phones and iPhones record VFR to save battery.
  • NVIDIA ShadowPlay / GeForce Experience captures: these frequently record VFR.
  • Meeting recordings (Zoom, Teams, Meet): the frame rate varies with available bandwidth.

The tell-tale sign of VFR is that the drift starts small and grows as the clip progresses. At 30 seconds the audio might be 0.2 s behind; at five minutes it can be 2–3 seconds behind.

Nudging the offset by hand does not work with VFR. Shift the audio track by a fixed number of frames and sync improves at one point in the clip while staying wrong everywhere else. The only real fix is to convert the video to CFR before importing.

Cause 3: an export mistake

Less common Drift only in the exported file

Everything sounds right in the DaVinci Resolve preview, but the exported MP4 or MOV has the audio out of sync. This usually comes down to an incompatible audio codec plus export setting combination, or to using hardware acceleration during the render.

This particular case is usually resolved with these settings on the Deliver page:

  • Change the Audio Codec from AAC to Linear PCM or FLAC.
  • Turn off video Hardware acceleration for the render.
  • Lower the video Data Rate if you are pushing your GPU to its limit.

Fixing VFR-related drift: convert to CFR

If the problem is a variable frame rate, you need to convert the video to a constant frame rate (CFR). That does mean re-encoding the video rather than remuxing, so there is some quality loss with a lossy codec. At -crf 18 that loss is visually imperceptible.

With FFmpeg, the command to convert VFR to CFR is:

ffmpeg -i video_vfr.mp4 -vf fps=30 -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -c:a flac video_cfr.mkv

Change fps=30 to your video's original frame rate — 25 for European PAL, 24 for cinema, 60 for gaming. If you do not know it, check with:

ffprobe -v quiet -show_streams -select_streams v:0 video.mp4 | grep r_frame_rate
VidToFLAC and VFR: VidToFLAC converts the audio to FLAC but copies the video without re-encoding (-c:v copy), which preserves the original VFR. If VFR is specifically your problem, you need FFmpeg in the terminal with the fps filter to force CFR. VidToFLAC resolves codec-related drift, but not VFR-related drift.

Working out which cause is yours

Before applying any fix, identify the cause with these three questions:

  1. 1 Does the drift grow through the clip? If the audio is 0.1 s behind at the start and 2 s behind at the end, it is VFR. Convert to CFR with FFmpeg.
  2. 2 Is the offset constant from the start? If the audio is always exactly the same amount ahead or behind, it is most likely a codec problem. Convert the audio to FLAC with VidToFLAC.
  3. 3 Fine in preview but wrong in the export? That is an export settings problem. Change the audio codec to Linear PCM or FLAC on the Deliver page, and turn off hardware acceleration.

If you cannot tell VFR from a codec problem, check the file with ffprobe:

ffprobe -v quiet -show_streams -select_streams a:0 video.mp4 | grep codec_name

If the result is aac, mp3 or ac3, the codec is the problem and VidToFLAC resolves it. If it comes back pcm_s16le or similar and the drift still grows, the problem is VFR.

Frequently asked questions

Why does audio drift out of sync in DaVinci Resolve?

The most common causes are: (1) an audio codec DaVinci Resolve does not decode cleanly (AAC, MP3, AC-3), especially on Linux; (2) video shot at a variable frame rate, typical of phones, OBS and screen recorders; (3) incorrect export settings. A constant offset points to the codec; drift that grows points to VFR.

How do I re-sync the audio by hand in DaVinci Resolve?

Select the audio track on the timeline and use Ctrl+Alt+left/right arrow to nudge it frame by frame. That only works if the offset is constant across the whole clip, though. If the drift grows over time (VFR), manual adjustment will not do: sync will be right at one point and wrong everywhere else. In that case, convert the file to CFR before importing.

Does the drift show up in the exported file too?

It depends where it starts. If the drift comes from the source file — codec or VFR — the export will carry it too, even where the preview looks fine. If you nudge the offset on the timeline without addressing the VFR, the exported file can come out wrong again. The right fix is to repair the source file before importing, not to patch it by hand.

Does VidToFLAC fix VFR-related audio drift?

VidToFLAC resolves drift caused by an incompatible audio codec (AAC, MP3, AC-3), which is the most common cause. For VFR-related drift you need to convert the video to a constant frame rate with FFmpeg using the fps filter. If your drift grows over time, VFR is the problem and that extra step is required.

If the audio is not just drifting but silent altogether, see the guide to why DaVinci Resolve will not import MP4 and OBS audio. For audio problems on export, see DaVinci Resolve rendering with no audio.
