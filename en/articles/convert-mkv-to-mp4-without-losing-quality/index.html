MKV and MP4: containers, not quality

The first misunderstanding to clear up: MKV and MP4 are containers, not codecs. A container is like an envelope — it can hold different kinds of content inside (video, audio and subtitle tracks), but it does not determine the quality of that content. Quality depends on the codec inside.

The same H.264 video at the same quality fits inside an MKV or inside an MP4 — the picture is identical either way. What changes is compatibility:

Feature MKV MP4
Device compatibility Medium — needs a compatible app or player High — native on phones, smart TVs and consoles
YouTube / social media Limited — YouTube accepts MKV, Instagram does not Broad — accepted on virtually every network
Audio codecs supported Broad — AAC, MP3, AC-3, FLAC, Opus, DTS… Limited — mainly AAC and MP3
Multiple tracks / subtitles Yes — multiple audio tracks, embedded subtitles Partial — limited; subtitles in a separate file
DaVinci Resolve (Linux) Yes — with FLAC or PCM audio Partial — video yes, AAC audio no on Linux
Professional editing generally Well supported Well supported

In short: if you need compatibility with every device and platform, MP4 wins. If you need codec flexibility and multiple tracks — or compatibility with DaVinci Resolve on Linux — MKV with FLAC is the better option.

What a remux is and why it loses nothing

A remux is the process of changing a video file's container without touching the track data. The H.264 video that was inside the MKV is copied across to the MP4 exactly as it was — not one byte of the picture data changes. The same goes for the audio.

That is the crucial difference from a conversion or re-encode, where the video is decoded frame by frame and compressed again. That does lose quality, because every compression cycle introduces further artefacts.

Remux vs. re-encode:
Remux → the container changes, the data does not. Zero quality loss. Seconds to process.
Re-encode → the video is decompressed and recompressed. The loss accumulates. It can take hours at 4K.

For the vast majority of MKV files — those carrying H.264 or H.265 video with AAC or MP3 audio — remuxing to MP4 is direct, instant and lossless.

When an MKV → MP4 remux just works

Remuxing MKV to MP4 works without trouble when the MKV's codecs are compatible with the MP4 container:

  • H.264 (AVC) video — the most common. Compatible with MP4. ✓
  • H.265 (HEVC) video — compatible with MP4 in most profiles. ✓
  • AAC audio — the MP4 standard. Compatible. ✓
  • MP3 audio — technically supported in MP4, though not ideal. ✓

If your MKV came from an OBS recording with H.264 and AAC, from a streaming download, or from an action camera, remuxing to MP4 will work straight away.

When it does not work, and what to do

A direct remux fails when the MKV contains codecs the MP4 container will not take:

Codec in the MKV MP4 compatible Fix
FLAC audio No Convert the audio to AAC before remuxing, or stay with MKV
Opus audio No Convert the audio to AAC, or stay with MKV
Vorbis audio No Convert the audio to AAC, or stay with MKV
AC-3 / DTS audio Partial Works in some players, but not all
VP8 / VP9 video No Needs a re-encode to H.264 or H.265
AV1 video Partial Supported in MP4, but poorly supported by players
Rule of thumb: if the MKV has H.264 or H.265 video with AAC or MP3 audio, remuxing to MP4 is direct and lossless. If it has FLAC, Opus or Vorbis audio, you are better off staying with MKV, or converting the audio to AAC for the MP4.

Converting with FFmpeg in the terminal

If you prefer the command line, FFmpeg is the standard tool. This command remuxes without touching the codecs — ideal when the MKV carries H.264 and AAC:

ffmpeg -i file.mkv -c copy output.mp4

If the MKV has FLAC or Opus audio, which MP4 will not take, you need to convert just the audio to AAC while copying the video:

ffmpeg -i file.mkv -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 192k output.mp4

If the MKV has VP9 or AV1 video, which players expecting H.264 inside an MP4 will not handle, you need to re-encode the video. There will be some loss, but a low CRF keeps it to a minimum:

ffmpeg -i file.mkv -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -c:a aac -b:a 192k output.mp4

To process several files at once in bash:

for f in *.mkv; do ffmpeg -i "$f" -c copy "${f%.mkv}.mp4"; done

MKV vs MP4 for DaVinci Resolve

If your end goal is editing in DaVinci Resolve, it is worth pausing before you settle on an output format:

  • DaVinci Resolve on Linux cannot decode AAC — the standard audio in MP4. If you convert to MP4 with AAC, you end up with exactly the audio problem you started with.
  • DaVinci Resolve on Windows, macOS and Linux reads FLAC natively. An MKV with FLAC audio is the most broadly compatible combination.
  • If you need MP4 for DaVinci Resolve — some workflows require it — use MP4 with FLAC audio, which is what VidToFLAC offers as an output option.

Frequently asked questions

Do you lose quality converting MKV to MP4?

No, not if you do it as a remux. A remux copies the video bit for bit without recompressing. Quality is only lost in a re-encode, which is only necessary when the MKV's codecs are not MP4-compatible — VP9 or AV1 for video, FLAC or Opus for audio.

Why can't my MKV be converted straight to MP4?

The usual reason is that the MKV carries audio in a codec MP4 does not accept: FLAC, Opus or Vorbis. The fix is to convert the audio to AAC at the same time as you change the container. With FFmpeg: ffmpeg -i file.mkv -c:v copy -c:a aac output.mp4. VidToFLAC handles this case automatically.

How long does converting MKV to MP4 take?

With a remux it depends on your disk's read and write speed, not on the CPU. A 10 GB MKV usually takes between 20 and 60 seconds. If the video has to be re-encoded, it can take 10–100× longer depending on resolution and length.

What is the quality difference between MKV and MP4?

None at all. MKV and MP4 are containers, not codecs. Quality depends on the video codec (H.264, H.265 and so on) and its compression settings (bitrate, CRF). The same H.264 video inside an MKV or inside an MP4 is identical in quality — only compatibility with players and platforms changes.

Is MKV or MP4 better for editing in DaVinci Resolve?

For editing in DaVinci Resolve, especially on Linux, MKV with FLAC audio is the better choice. The free DaVinci Resolve on Linux cannot decode the AAC audio that most MP4 files carry. FLAC is open source and DaVinci Resolve supports it on Windows, macOS and Linux. If you need an MP4 to share, keep the MKV for editing and export to MP4 from DaVinci Resolve when you are done.
