Why audio loses quality when exporting from DaVinci Resolve

When you export a video from DaVinci Resolve, the program recompresses the audio by default. If your project's audio was already compressed as AAC or MP3 and you export to AAC or MP3 again, each compression cycle adds new artefacts and degrades quality cumulatively. This is known as generational recompression.

On top of that, if the project's audio settings — sample rate, bit depth — do not match the source material, DaVinci Resolve performs a conversion that can introduce subtle quality differences or, in extreme cases, sync drift.

The most common causes of audio quality loss on export are:

  • Exporting AAC or MP3 when the original was PCM or FLAC — you introduce lossy compression where there was none.
  • Too low an audio bitrate — AAC at 128 kbps sounds noticeably worse than at 320 kbps.
  • A sample rate mismatch — the project runs at 44,100 Hz while the material is 48,000 Hz, or the other way round.
  • Generational recompression — the original audio was already AAC and gets compressed to AAC again on export.
  • Unwanted normalisation — DaVinci Resolve applies automatic level adjustment that alters your mix's dynamics.

DaVinci Resolve's Deliver page: the audio settings explained

The Deliver page is where every export setting lives. To reach the audio controls, open the Audio section in the render settings panel. The settings that matter:

1
Codec

Choose the compression algorithm. The options that matter are FLAC (lossless, compressed), PCM (uncompressed), AAC (lossy) and MP3 (lossy). For maximum quality use FLAC: the same quality as PCM in 40 to 60% less space.

2
Data Rate / Bitrate

Only appears with lossy codecs (AAC, MP3). The higher the bitrate, the better the quality. For internet distribution, 320 kbps is the practical maximum with AAC.

3
Sample Rate

Always use 48,000 Hz for video — it is the film and television standard. Only use 44,100 Hz if the destination is purely music or podcasts.

4
Bit Depth

With FLAC, choose 24-bit for masters and 16-bit for distribution. With AAC or MP3 the setting does not apply, since the codec uses its own quantisation.

5
Channels

Stereo for most projects. 5.1 or 7.1 if you are mixing surround. Mono only where the destination explicitly calls for it, as some podcasts do.

One option to turn off: some presets enable "Normalize Audio". If your mix is already mastered at the right levels, switch it off — automatic normalisation can alter your mix's dynamics.

Which audio codec to choose when exporting from DaVinci Resolve

Which audio codec to export depends on where the video is going. Here is the comparison:

Codec Quality Size Best for
PCM / WAV Master Lossless Large Master files, client delivery, post-production
FLAC Master Lossless Medium Compressed master files, lossless MKV
AAC 320 kbps Distribution High (lossy) Small YouTube, Vimeo, streaming, social media
AAC 128–192 kbps Acceptable Medium (lossy) Very small Web and mobile, where size matters more than quality
MP3 Avoid for video Medium (lossy) Small Audio-only and podcasts — not recommended for video
The golden rule: if the final destination is going to recompress the audio anyway (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok), export AAC at 320 kbps — there is no practical benefit to FLAC if the platform will convert it regardless. Keep the FLAC for your master.

The best audio export settings for each destination

Exporting for YouTube

YouTube re-encodes everything you upload, so exporting at wildly high quality does not improve the end result on the platform. The best audio settings are:

  • Codec: AAC
  • Bitrate: 320 kbps
  • Sample rate: 48,000 Hz
  • Channels: stereo

Use DaVinci Resolve's "YouTube" preset as a starting point and set the audio bitrate to 320 kbps if it is not there already.

Exporting for client delivery or a master file

Here exporting losslessly genuinely matters. The client, or the next link in the post-production chain, needs maximum quality in case further editing is required.

  • Codec: FLAC
  • Bit depth: 24-bit
  • Sample rate: 48,000 Hz
  • Container: MOV or MKV (for FLAC)

Exporting for Instagram or TikTok

These platforms recompress aggressively. Instagram caps resolution at 1080p and re-encodes to H.264 with AAC audio. There is no point exporting at maximum quality — export AAC at 256 kbps with H.264, which is what the platform will produce anyway. You save render time and disk space with no effect on the published quality.

Exporting for Premiere Pro, Avid or another editor

If the exported file is going into other editing software — a multi-tool workflow — always export 24-bit FLAC at 48,000 Hz. It is the most compact lossless format and works with every modern professional editor.

Common audio export mistakes in DaVinci Resolve, and how to fix them

The exported audio sounds different from the original

The most frequent cause is generational recompression. If your project's audio was already AAC and you export to AAC, the result will sound worse because two lossy compression cycles have been applied. The fix: export FLAC for the master and keep an AAC copy for distribution only.

The exported audio is out of sync

Two main causes. First, the source material was Variable Frame Rate — smartphone video in automatic mode varies its frame rate, which throws off audio sync on export. Second, a sample rate mismatch between the project and the material. Check that your DaVinci Resolve project is set to the same sample rate as your material (File → Project Settings → Master Settings → Audio sample rate).

If the material was Variable Frame Rate: convert it to Constant Frame Rate with FFmpeg before importing into DaVinci Resolve: ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vsync cfr -c:v copy -c:a flac output.mkv. That removes the root cause of the drift.

The exported video has no audio

This happens when the project's audio track is muted, when its volume is at zero, or when DaVinci Resolve has lost the link to the original audio file — it shows red in the Media Pool. Check that the audio track is active on the timeline and that every clip shows its audio icon before exporting.

The exported file is too large

FLAC compresses audio by 40 to 60% compared with PCM, without discarding any data. On a two-hour video that difference can run to several gigabytes — always use FLAC rather than PCM for the master.

Prepare the audio before importing into DaVinci Resolve for better results

The quality of the audio you export depends heavily on the quality of the audio going in. If you import video with AAC, MP3 or Opus audio, DaVinci Resolve works with that compressed audio throughout the edit — and any further processing is applied to data that is already degraded.

The recommended practice is to convert the audio to FLAC before importing the material into DaVinci Resolve. The editor then works with lossless audio from the outset, and the final export preserves all the quality available.

Frequently asked questions about audio export in DaVinci Resolve

How do I export from DaVinci Resolve without losing audio quality?

On the Deliver page, in the Audio section, choose the FLAC codec — lossless but compressed: the same quality as PCM in 40 to 60% less space. Set the sample rate to 48,000 Hz and the bit depth to 24-bit for maximum quality.

Why does audio lose quality when exporting from DaVinci Resolve?

Because DaVinci Resolve recompresses the audio on export. If the original audio was already AAC and you export to AAC, you apply two lossy compression cycles — generational recompression. The fix is to always export FLAC for the master file.

What is the best audio codec for exporting to YouTube from DaVinci Resolve?

AAC at 320 kbps is plenty, and it is the standard YouTube accepts. The platform recompresses everything on upload anyway, so exporting FLAC does not improve the result on YouTube. Keep the FLAC as your master copy.

Can DaVinci Resolve export FLAC?

Yes. On the Deliver page, select the MKV container and choose the FLAC codec in the Audio section. DaVinci Resolve will export lossless FLAC audio inside the MKV file.

Why is the audio out of sync in my DaVinci Resolve export?

The usual causes are that the source material was Variable Frame Rate, typical of smartphone video, or a sample rate mismatch between the project and the material. For VFR, convert the file to CFR before importing: ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vsync cfr -c:v copy -c:a flac output.mkv

Which sample rate should I use when exporting from DaVinci Resolve?

48,000 Hz is the standard for all video content — film, television and streaming platforms. Only use 44,100 Hz if the destination is audio-only (CD, music platforms). The wrong rate can cause small shifts in the audio's pitch.
