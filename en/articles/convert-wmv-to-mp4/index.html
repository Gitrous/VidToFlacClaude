What WMV is and why you need to convert it

WMV (Windows Media Video) is a video format Microsoft developed in the late 1990s. It uses the VC-1 codec (or WMV3) for video and WMA (Windows Media Audio) for audio, both proprietary to Microsoft.

It was popular in its day because Windows Media Player played it natively on any Windows PC. Today the drawbacks are obvious:

  • macOS: QuickTime will not play WMV without extra plugins. You need VLC, or you convert the file.
  • Linux: WMV needs proprietary codecs that most distributions do not ship.
  • Phones: neither iOS nor Android normally plays WMV natively; an extra app is usually needed.
  • Video editors: in DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro and Final Cut, WMV support is limited or absent outside Windows, and depends on the version and the codecs installed.
  • Web platforms: YouTube accepts WMV, but Instagram, TikTok and most others do not.

The conclusion is clear: MP4 with H.264 has the widest compatibility. WMV is all but Windows-only, and even there it is not the preferred format for editing or publishing.

A note on timing: WMV needs a full re-encode (not a remux), so it is slower than MKV or MP4. For a 30-minute 720p WMV, expect somewhere between 5 and 15 minutes depending on your machine. For long meeting recordings (1-2 hours) it can take 20-40 minutes.

The FFmpeg alternative in the terminal

If you already have FFmpeg installed (it runs on Windows, Mac and Linux), this command converts WMV to MP4 at high quality:

ffmpeg -i video.wmv -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -c:a aac -b:a 192k output.mp4

To keep the audio lossless (ideal if you will edit afterwards):

ffmpeg -i video.wmv -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -c:a flac output.mkv

To convert every WMV in a folder:

for f in *.wmv; do ffmpeg -i "$f" -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset slow -c:a aac "${f%.wmv}.mp4"; done

WMV in DaVinci Resolve, Premiere and other editors

If your goal is to import the WMV into a video editor, here is what you need to know:

DaVinci Resolve

On Windows, DaVinci Resolve can open WMV if the Windows Media codecs are installed, but support is inconsistent and certain WMV profiles cause trouble. On Linux and macOS, DaVinci Resolve cannot open WMV at all. Convert to MKV with H.264 video and FLAC audio for dependable compatibility on every system.

Adobe Premiere Pro

Premiere Pro on Windows supports WMV through Windows Media Foundation. On macOS it does not, unless you install additional plugins. For cross-platform workflows it is safer to convert to MP4 or MKV.

Final Cut Pro

Final Cut Pro only runs on macOS and does not support WMV at all. Convert to MP4 with H.264 before importing.

Frequently asked questions

Do you lose quality converting WMV to MP4?

A little, because WMV to MP4 requires re-encoding the video — a direct remux is not possible. At CRF 18 with H.264 the loss is visually imperceptible; that is the standard setting for high-quality material. The FLAC audio is lossless. You will not see the difference from the original WMV on a normal screen.

Why can't I play my WMV on a Mac or iPhone?

WMV uses Microsoft's VC-1 and WMA codecs, which are not included natively in macOS or iOS. Neither QuickTime nor the iPhone video player supports them. Convert the WMV to MP4 with H.264 and you get a file that plays natively on any Mac or iPhone with nothing to install.

Can I convert WMV to MP4 without installing any software?

Yes. VidToFLAC converts WMV directly in the browser using FFmpeg WebAssembly. There is nothing to install — no FFmpeg, no HandBrake, no external converter. Open the tool, drop in the WMV and download the result. The file never leaves your computer.

Can I import WMV directly into DaVinci Resolve?

Only on Windows, and with caveats. On Linux and macOS, DaVinci Resolve cannot open WMV. Even on Windows, some WMV profiles cause problems. For a workflow without surprises, convert the WMV to MKV with H.264 and FLAC audio using VidToFLAC — that combination works in DaVinci Resolve on Windows, macOS and Linux.
