Quick diagnosis: is it an import or an export problem?

Before hunting for fixes, work out where the audio is being lost:

  • Can you see a waveform on the timeline and hear audio when you play back with the spacebar? Then it is an export problem — the audio reaches DaVinci Resolve fine but is not included in the render. Read on from Cause 1.
  • No waveform on the timeline, or silence on playback? Then it is an import problem — DaVinci Resolve cannot read the source file's audio. Convert the audio to FLAC with VidToFLAC before importing.
The shortcut test: in DaVinci Resolve, go to the Edit page and hit the spacebar over a clip with audio. If you see the blue waveform on the audio track and hear sound, the audio is inside DaVinci Resolve and the problem is on export. If the track is empty or flat, the problem happened before DaVinci Resolve ever saw it.

Cause 1: "Export Audio" is off in Deliver

This is the most common cause, and the easiest to overlook. On DaVinci Resolve's Deliver page, the settings panel has an Audio section with an "Export Audio" checkbox. If it is unticked, the render produces video with no audio track at all.

How to check and fix it:

  1. Go to the Deliver page — the rocket icon in the bottom bar
  2. In the settings panel on the left, find the Audio section
  3. Make sure the Export Audio box is ticked
  4. Check too that the selected audio codec is compatible — see Cause 3
Worth knowing: if you use a custom export preset, or one you saved earlier, it may have audio disabled by default. Always check the audio settings before adding the job to the render queue.

Cause 2: muted or disabled audio tracks

DaVinci Resolve has several layers of muting that can keep audio out of the render even with "Export Audio" enabled:

Tracks muted on the Edit page

On the Edit page timeline, every audio track has an M (mute) button. If it is active — shown in red or orange — that track will not be included in the render. Make sure no audio track has M enabled.

Tracks disabled in Fairlight

If you use the Fairlight page for mixing, each channel has its own enable button. A track can be disabled in Fairlight without that being visible on the Edit page. Go to Fairlight and check that every track that should be heard is active.

A timeline with no audio track assigned

In projects with multiple timelines, make sure the one you are exporting has the right audio tracks and is not pointing at a nested timeline that has none.

Cause 3: audio codec incompatible with the container

Not every audio codec fits in every video container. When there is a mismatch, DaVinci Resolve may produce a file with no audio, or with an empty audio track, without showing any visible error.

The most common problem combinations:

  • MP4 with FLAC audio: MP4 does not take FLAC natively. Choose FLAC as the audio codec with MP4 as the container and you may end up with a silent MP4.
  • MP4 with Opus audio: Opus is not compatible with MP4 either. Use AAC, MP3 or PCM for MP4.
  • MOV with an incompatible codec: Apple's MOV takes AAC and PCM, but can misbehave with others.
The safest MP4 setup: on the Deliver page, pick H.264 Master as the base preset. In the Audio section, choose AAC as the codec and 320 kbps as the bitrate. That combination works everywhere.

Cause 4: the AAC problem on Linux

This is the most specific cause and the least documented. DaVinci Resolve on Linux ships no AAC encoder, for the same licensing reasons it ships no decoder. Try to export an MP4 with AAC audio from the free DaVinci Resolve on Linux and you get an MP4 with no audio, or with a silent audio track.

What to do on Linux:

  • Export with FLAC audio — available on every platform, Linux included
  • Export with PCM / Linear PCM audio — uncompressed WAV, also available
  • Use MKV as the container instead of MP4 — MKV takes FLAC and PCM without trouble
  • If you need MP4 with AAC for publishing, export MKV with FLAC from DaVinci Resolve first, then convert to MP4+AAC with FFmpeg: ffmpeg -i output.mkv -c:v copy -c:a aac final.mp4

Cause 5: the source file has no compatible audio

Sometimes the audio is on the DaVinci Resolve timeline, but DaVinci Resolve is reading it without being able to decode it properly. The waveform may look flat, or playback may be silent in the internal monitor, even though the clip is sitting there on the timeline.

This happens when the source file has an audio codec DaVinci Resolve cannot decode — AAC on Linux, for instance — but the clip drags onto the timeline anyway. The clip exists; the audio has been silent from the start.

How to spot it: zoom in on the clip's audio track on the Edit page. If the waveform is completely flat — a straight line with no variation — the audio is not being decoded, and the source file has a compatibility problem.

Frequently asked questions

Why does DaVinci Resolve export without audio when the timeline has sound?

The usual causes, most to least common: (1) the "Export Audio" box is unticked on the Deliver page; (2) an audio track is muted, with M active, on the Edit or Fairlight page; (3) the chosen audio codec is not compatible with the output container; (4) you are on Linux trying to export AAC, which DaVinci Resolve cannot encode without a licence.

DaVinci Resolve on Linux exports MP4 with no audio. What do I do?

DaVinci Resolve on Linux cannot encode AAC audio, the usual choice for MP4. Your options: (1) export MKV with FLAC audio from DaVinci Resolve; (2) if you need MP4, export MKV first and convert afterwards with ffmpeg -i output.mkv -c:v copy -c:a aac output.mp4.

The audio waveform in DaVinci Resolve is flat. What does that mean?

A completely flat waveform — a straight line with no variation — means DaVinci Resolve cannot decode the source file's audio. The clip is on the timeline, but the audio is silence. It happens when the file has an incompatible audio codec, such as AAC on Linux. The fix: convert the audio to FLAC with VidToFLAC before importing.

What are the safest audio export settings in DaVinci Resolve?

For the broadest compatibility: MP4 with AAC at 320 kbps, which does not work on Linux. For Linux, or to preserve maximum quality: MKV with FLAC. To share with someone who has no video editor: MP4 with AAC. For professional delivery or moving between programs: MKV with FLAC or PCM.
